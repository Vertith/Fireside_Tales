# Emit Time — Alternate Endings

**Status:** Ideas only, nothing implemented. Every ending here is a **SUGGESTION** built on the canon in [story-bible.md](story-bible.md) and [visitor-tells.md](visitor-tells.md).
**The brief:** endings that finish the game some way *other* than another reset. The canon true ending (shoot Emit right after the first flash, with the evidence in the Notebook) stays as it is. These sit beside it.

**One rule that makes them all work:** an ending only happens when the Tether (Emit's stolen recall watch, bible 5a) can't fire, or has nothing worth firing for. Each ending below is a different way of getting there.

## Summary

| # | Ending | Tone | How you get it | Emit's fate | Kane's fate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Custody** | Best / clean | Shut down the Tether, then let the warden through | Arrested alive | Saved |
| 2 | **Kane's Choice** | Bittersweet | Get the real Kane on the phone and tell him the truth | Stranded, powerless | Burns his own work |

Suggested order of difficulty: 5 (easiest, a mid-game "quit"), then 3 and 4 (late game, from trusting Emit), then 2, then 1 (hardest, needs the most loop knowledge). The canon true ending sits between 2 and 1.

## 1. Custody — "Prison issue, stolen."

**The idea:** the non-lethal win. The guard doesn't kill Emit; he takes away Emit's escape hatch and lets the people hunting him do their job.

**How you get it:**
- The player has learned three things: the watch reads `12.03.40` (2240), the warden is telling the truth (#30), and the shutdown device made Emit flinch (bible 5, clue 5).
- On a loop where the warden shows up, the player first uses the **shutdown device on the watch itself** when Emit comes down for "hold them off a bit longer." This is a new zoom target: the device does nothing to Emit (he's human) but kills the Tether. He doesn't notice, or he notices too late.
- Then the player lets the warden through. For the first time, letting an intruder through doesn't reset.

**What the player sees:**
1. The warden walks past the desk. A long silence. The lamp hums.
2. Emit runs down the stairs, slapping at his wrist. *"Come on. Come on."* Nothing happens.
3. The warden catches him at the door. Emit looks at the guard with real surprise, the first time he's ever looked at him like a person.
4. The warden's line on the way out: *"Recall anchor. Prison issue, stolen. You didn't shoot him. Most don't think of that."*
5. Morning. Kane walks out, as in the canon epilogue. The tally on the desk stays.

**Why it's worth having:** it rewards the player who understood the *mechanism* rather than the culprit. It also uses the shutdown device for its last and best job, which pays off the bible's twist hook.

**Open question:** is this the best ending, or equal to the canon one? *Default:* a "perfect" ending above the canon win, since it needs everything the canon one needs plus the device trick.

## 2. Kane's Choice — "Some doors you don't build."

**The idea:** the guard goes around Emit entirely and reaches the one person who has never come to the desk: the real Aldous Kane.

**How you get it:**
- The player has the mother's photo of the real Kane (visitor-tells #21) and the sister's description (#5), so they know the man who ran in isn't him.
- A late-game Rolodex unlock: Kane's real lab extension, learned from the sister or the janitor on an earlier loop. The guard calls it.
- A voice the player has never heard answers. A short dialogue tree: the guard tells Kane what his machine brings (the assassin, the hunters, the man wearing his name). Kane only believes him if the guard can name things only the loop could teach him (what Kane's mother is bringing, his childhood nickname).

**What the player sees:**
1. Kane goes quiet on the line. Then: *"How many times have you done this?"* The player's real loop count is offered as an answer.
2. A flash from upstairs, but a small, dying one. Kane is wrecking the machine and his notes himself.
3. Emit comes down the stairs. He tries the Tether. It works, but there's nothing to anchor to: time travel never gets finished, so there's no future to return from and no past to send him back to. The night just carries on.
4. Emit is left as an ordinary man in the 90s with a dead watch and no name.
5. **Epilogue beat:** years later, a new night guard sits at the desk. An older Emit pushes a mop past him, wearing a Science Corp janitor's badge. He glances at the watch out of habit.

**Why it's worth having:** it's the only ending where the world's choice is made by the inventor, not the guard. It's bittersweet: the hunters were right that Kane's work was important, and it's gone anyway.

**Paradox note:** this deliberately leaves one loose thread (how does Emit exist if time travel never does?). Noir is fine with that. The guard can even say it: *"Don't ask me how a man stays when his tomorrow's gone. I just work the desk."*

## 3. Destroy Time Travel — "Lightning, I'd told myself. I still do."

**The idea:** Emit's ending. The player believes him, because the bible says the player will want to.

**How you get it:**
- Reach the endgame without having triggered the twist (or ignoring it), and hold the desk against every intruder through to the end.
- When Emit comes down with "the only way to stop this is to destroy time travel," say yes.
- It can also trigger if the player has the evidence and *still* says yes, which gets a sharper last line.

**What the player sees:**
1. The flashes outside stop. The rain stops with them.
2. Emit thanks the guard, sincerely, and walks out the front door into the dark.
3. Morning, but the lobby is wrong in small ways: the portrait on the wall is Emit's face (the coin king from the past-era clues, bible 5, clue 2a). The Rolodex has no card for Kane. The sister walks in and doesn't know anyone named Aldous.
4. Only the guard remembers it was ever different. The static on his neck is gone.
5. Last narration: *"Lightning, I'd told myself. I still do. It's easier."* (If the player had the poster, it ends instead on: *"I knew his name. I said yes anyway."*)

**Why it's worth having:** a proper bad ending that recasts the whole game, the way *Papers, Please* does with its loyalist endings. It also gives weight to the canon win: this is what you stopped.

## 4. The Right Hand — "He offered me a crown. I took the coat."

**The idea:** a darker version of 3. Emit has been using this night as a safe base between his crimes. He's used the guard every loop. In this ending, he asks him to come along.

**How you get it:**
- The player has seen enough of Emit's crimes to know what he is (the coin, the history book that changes, the poster).
- At the "hold off a bit longer" scene, a loop-locked line opens only if the player has the poster: *"Evening, Emit."* (visitor-tells, the scientist). In canon he stops dead. Here, if the player doesn't then shoot or shut him down, he smiles and makes an offer: *"You've held the door longer than anyone. Come with me. You'll never stand guard again."*
- Accept.

**What the player sees:**
1. Emit takes off his scorched coat and puts it around the guard's shoulders. The future stitching catches the lamp light.
2. They walk out together. The guard's hand leaves the desk; the tally marks stay behind.
3. A short montage in the three-tone flash style (style guide 7): a throne room, a Roman forum, a 1920s ballroom. The guard is in the background of each one, standing at a door. Still guarding.
4. Last narration: *"Turned out the job was the same everywhere. Just a nicer coat."*

**Why it's worth having:** it reuses the past-era visitors' story (the people Emit wronged) and lets the player join the side the hunters were chasing. It also lands the noir theme: the guard never stops being a guard.

## 5. Off the Clock — "Not my building. Not my night."

**The idea:** the player refuses to play. The quitter's ending, short and a little haunting, like the "leave" endings in *The Stanley Parable*.

**How you get it:**
- After a set number of loops (say 5), a new option appears on the desk: the guard's coat on the hook. Click it and the guard can walk out the front door at any point after the first flash.
- A confirmation in the guard's voice: *"Fifteen years at this desk. They don't pay me enough for forever."*

**What the player sees:**
1. The guard walks out into the rain. Behind him, through the glass, the next flash lights up the lobby. He doesn't turn around.
2. Black screen. The sound of a radio.
3. His apartment, morning. The radio says there was an "electrical fire" at Science Corp overnight. No names.
4. He looks at his hand. Faint scratches on his palm, like a tally, that he doesn't remember making.
5. Last narration: *"Whatever was happening in that lobby, it's somebody else's shift now."*

**Lore reason it doesn't reset:** the guard only remembers because he stands nearest the anchor (bible 5a). If he leaves, the loop may still be running, but it no longer runs *through him*. He's out, and the player never finds out who wins.

**Why it's worth having:** an honest exit for players who are worn down by the loop, and a hint that someone else may be stuck there now. It could also unlock a line from *Future you* (#25) on a new game: *"You walked out once. I remember. I didn't."*

## Open questions for Adam

1. **Which of these to keep?** *Default:* all five, with Custody as a hidden "perfect" ending.
2. **Does the game end after an alternate ending,** or return the player to the title with the Notebook kept? *Default:* each ending shows its epilogue and a final tally, then returns to the title. A small ending gallery on the title screen shows which ones the player has found.
3. **Should the bad endings (3 and 4) be reachable by accident on a first playthrough?** *Default:* 3 yes, since it's what a trusting player would naturally do. 4 no, it needs the poster.
4. **New mechanics these need:** a shutdown-device zoom target on the watch (1), a Kane phone extension in the Rolodex (2), and a coat-on-the-hook action (5). Endings 3 and 4 use what already exists.
