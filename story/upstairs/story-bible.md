# Upstairs — Story Bible

**Status:** Draft v1 (14 Oct 2026). Built from Adam's main-loop outline.
**How to read this:** **CANON** is Adam's outline, kept as written. **SUGGESTION** is a proposed default the first playable build uses; change or drop any of it.

---

## 1. Premise — CANON

It's the early 2000s. You are a young child who has just finished getting ready for bed. Your mother sits in your room and reads you a book, a Bram Stoker style tale about a child abducted by the Fae when he wanders into the woods. She gets a few lines out before your eyes close and you fall asleep.

You wake up. The room is dimmer. The book and your mother's shawl are lying in the rocking chair she was sitting in, and there is a strange noise downstairs.

You navigate the house while creatures imitate your mother, your father, your pets, a police officer and others. Eventually you find your real family hidden in the basement.

The story is the point. Small puzzles (a cut phone cord, a ripped-up photo of the dog, the basement key) are a minor part.

## 1a. Setting and tone — SUGGESTION

- **When:** October 2003. Never stated on screen beyond small details: a corded kitchen phone with a long curly cord and an answering machine, a CRT TV on a stand, a blinking `12:00` alarm clock, a Game Boy on the nightstand, glow-in-the-dark stars, a flip phone on the counter, a VHS tape of *Homeward Bound*.
- **Where:** an old two-storey house the Hale family moved into today. Tonight is their first night in it, boxes everywhere (Adam, 14 Oct). Woods behind it. Bedroom, hall, bathroom and parents' room upstairs; foyer, living room and kitchen downstairs; a basement under the kitchen; an attic over the hall.
- **Look:** Fireside's night palette and one warm light: the child's flashlight (an old yellow-bulb one from under the bed). Before the flashlight, only the plug-in nightlight. The accent is **candle violet** (`#2e1e3a` `#6a4a8a` `#c8a8e8`), and it only ever means *one of them*: a glint in an eye, a candle flame in the attic, the light under a door.
- **Voice:** the child narrates in first person, past tense, in short plain sentences a seven-year-old would think in, with the odd big word borrowed from grown-ups. Scary because it's matter-of-fact. *"Mom's shawl was on my chair. So I didn't know what Mom was wearing."*
- **Horror level:** dread, not gore. Deaths cut to black on the moment of contact. The shotgun ending is one sound and a short line.

## 3. The Fae — SUGGESTION (the rules everything hangs on)

The book Mom reads lays these rules out, and the house keeps proving them. Every tell in the game comes from one of them, so a player who learns the rules can read any fake.

1. **They are copied from the mirror side of things.** Everything about them is the wrong way round: Dad's watch on the right wrist, Mom's hair parted on the wrong side, the deputy's badge number reading backwards, Biscuit's bone tag pointing the wrong way. They have no reflection.
2. **They cannot tell a plain lie.** Ask "Are you my mom?" and the fake says *"I'm the one who tucked you in,"* never *"Yes."*
3. **They cannot abide cold iron.** Dad nailed old horseshoes over the basement door and laid a ring of iron (nails, the cast-iron pan, a tyre chain) round the family below. That is why the family is safe down there and why the fakes can't follow.
4. **They want an answer.** If they call you and you go to them, or let them touch you, or open a door to them, you're theirs. (This is the "interact directly and you die" rule.)
5. **They don't quite know colours in the dark.** They get small things wrong: Biscuit's collar is blue on the fake.
6. **They only know what they've overheard.** They know the words of the house but not its habits. The fake Mom says "sweetheart". The fake Dad has heard "bug" and uses it, so words alone don't decide; look too.

The candle-violet glint in the eyes is the last, faint tell, and only shows now and then.

## 4. Why the night repeats — SUGGESTION

Every death sends Owen back to waking in his bed at 3:07, the rocking chair, the shawl, the noise downstairs. The notebook (his crayon notebook) keeps what he learned, like a dream you half remember.

The reason is in the attic. The Whitlocks drew a circle to bring Thomas back: "the night told over and over until he comes home". It never brought Thomas back, but it's still working, so the house replays any night the Fae come for a child. The attic ending reveals this; the other endings don't need it.

## 5. Story beats — the night, in order

### 5.1 Bedtime (prologue) — CANON, with SUGGESTION lines

- 8:40 PM. Owen in bed, Mom in the rocking chair with the shawl round her shoulders, the book open. Biscuit's tags jingle at the foot of the bed.
- Dad leans in: "Furnace is knocking again. I locked the basement, key's up on the frame." He taps his watch (left wrist). "Lights out by nine, bug."
- Mom reads from *The Hollow Hill*, written as a Victorian clergyman's journal:
  > *From the journal of the Reverend Josiah Penhallow. 3rd October.* The boy went into the beech-wood at dusk, against every warning his mother gave him, and the thing that walked out of it at moonrise wore his face as a man wears a borrowed coat.
  >
  > It sat at her table. It ate nothing. It called her Mother, in a voice that had been listening at keyholes a long, long while.
  >
  > Mark me well. They cannot abide cold iron. They cannot speak a plain lie. And they are made from the mirror's side of things, so that all of them is turned the wrong way about...
- Owen's eyes close on "the wrong way about", in a few slow, heavy blinks (Adam: a slow blink, not the Fireside flash). He wakes the same way.

### 5.2 Waking — CANON

- 3:07 AM. The room dimmer, the nightlight the only light. The book and the shawl in the empty rocking chair. A noise downstairs: something dragging, then the TV turning on to static.
- On later loops the narration knows it's been here before ("The clock said 3:07 again. I knew the noise before it came.").

### 5.3 The house — SUGGESTION (the map and who's where)

```
UPSTAIRS   Bedroom ── Hall ── Bathroom (stepstool, mirror: the other Owen)
                       │  └── Parents' room (fake Mom; closet with the attic pole)
                       ├── Attic hatch (needs the pole) ── Attic (circle, journal)
                       └── Stairs
DOWNSTAIRS Foyer (front door: the deputies; basement door: horseshoes, key on the frame)
             ├── Living room (fake Dad in the armchair, TV static; the torn photo)
             └── Kitchen (cut phone cord; fake Biscuit; Mrs. Albright at the back door)
BASEMENT   Down from the foyer once unlocked: the real family inside the iron ring
```

- **Bedroom.** The book (reading on gives the rest of the rules: *"and if they call you by your name, child, do not answer, for answering is the door"*). The shawl is cold: Mom's been gone a long time. The flashlight under the bed.
- **Hall.** Pepper sits outside the parents' door, back arched. Mom's voice from inside: *"Sweetheart? Come here, sweetheart."*
- **Parents' room.** The fake Mom sits up in bed. You can back out, or hide in the closet while she looks for you; hiding there finds the attic pole. She gives up and goes down the hall.
- **Bathroom.** Owen's stepstool. In the mirror, Owen is there... and then he isn't, and the boy in the glass smiles a beat late. (A fake, met through glass: touching the mirror kills.) This is where the player learns *they have no reflection* and *everything in a mirror is backwards*.
- **Foyer.** The basement door is locked; two horseshoes over it. Standing on the stepstool, Owen can feel along the top of the frame and find the key.
- **Living room.** The fake Dad in the armchair, lit by static. *"Come sit with me, bug."* His watch is on his right wrist. The family photo that was on the mantel is torn up on the rug.
- **Kitchen.** The phone on the wall, its curly cord cut. The fake Biscuit at an empty bowl, eating nothing, not breathing. Later, Mrs. Albright taps on the back door glass: *"Your mother asked me to look in on you, dear. Open up."*
- **Front door.** If you haven't called, a fake deputy knocks: *"Somebody called about a disturbance."* Nobody did; the phone was cut. If you have called, the fake comes first (they listened on the upstairs extension), and the real deputy comes after.
- **Basement.** A camping lantern, the iron ring, Dad with the shotgun at the bottom of the stairs, Mom behind him, Biscuit. *"Stop right there. You've come down those stairs three times tonight, and every time it wasn't you."*

### 5.4 Convincing the family — SUGGESTION

Dad won't let Owen past the bottom step. Owen has to prove he's the real one. Each test only counts if the player brings the knowledge with them; guessing is how you get shot.

| Test | The real answer | Where you learn it |
|---|---|---|
| Dad: "What colour is Biscuit's collar?" | Red | The torn photo puzzle (the fakes all said blue) |
| Mom: "Where did I stop reading?" | "The wrong way about" | The prologue (Owen fell asleep on it) |
| Owen: "Look at me in a mirror." | Mom's compact shows his reflection | The bathroom mirror teaches that they have none |
| Owen: "I'll step over the iron." | He steps over the horseshoe line | The book's rules and the horseshoes over the door |

Two good proofs and Dad lowers the gun. One wrong answer gets "Last chance, bug." A second wrong answer, or running out of proofs, gets the shotgun ending. Calling for Mom to *come here* is direct interaction with no proof yet: Dad fires.

## 6. The five endings — CANON, with SUGGESTION details

| # | Ending | How you get it | Card |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Taken** (many deaths) | Go to, touch, answer or open a door to any doppelganger. Each one is its own short death and resets the night. | Cut to black, their violet eyes open in the dark one pair at a time, then "Mom wasn't Mom." / "Maybe it was all a dream, but it felt so real." |
| 2 | **Shot** | Reach the basement and fail to convince Dad you're the real son. | *"Dad had been right three times tonight. He didn't wait to see if he was wrong."* |
| 3 | **Reunited** | Reach the basement and convince them. Dawn comes; the things outside the iron go quiet. If you called 911, the deputy's lights come up the drive at sunrise. | *Morning.* |
| 4 | **Outside** | Call 911, get past the fake deputy, and go with the real one. He takes you to the car "and we'll go back for your folks." The house is empty when they search it. You never see your family again. | *The house was empty.* |
| 5 | **The Hollow Hill** | Reach the attic and step into the Whitlocks' circle. The candles light violet on their own, the window opens on the beech-wood, and Owen walks the way Thomas did while Mom's voice reads the end of the book. | *He went into the beech-wood.* |

Deaths are resets, not end cards; the four others are end cards. The end card shows how many of the four you've found.

## 7. Doppelganger tells — SUGGESTION

Each fake has 3–4 things you can look at; one or two are real tells sitting among ordinary details.

| Fake | Where | Tells | Ordinary details | What going to them does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mom** | Parents' room | Wearing a shawl, but Mom's shawl is on Owen's chair. Calls him "sweetheart", never "bug". Hair parted on the wrong side. Won't say "I'm your mom." | Her slippers, the bedside lamp off, the alarm clock blinking 12:00. | She hugs him. Her arms are cold and much longer than arms. |
| **Dad** | Living room armchair | Watch on his right wrist. Glasses don't reflect the static. Knows "bug", so the words alone don't give him away. | Remote in his hand, his flannel shirt, the empty beer bottle. | He pats the arm of the chair. The chair is not a chair. |
| **Biscuit** | Kitchen | Blue collar (the photo shows red). Tag faces the wrong way. No breath fog, no panting, eats from an empty bowl. | Wagging tail, muddy paws. | He licks Owen's hand. His tongue is cold as a spoon. |
| **Mrs. Albright** | Kitchen back door | It's 3 AM. She's barefoot. Her house is dark behind her. She asks to be let in. | Curlers, housecoat, her reading glasses on a chain. | Owen unlocks the door. |
| **The deputy (fake)** | Front door | Nobody called (the cord was cut), or he knows about the call because they were on the extension. Badge number backwards. Dry hat in the rain. Says "you have to say I can come in." | Radio on his belt (silent), flashlight. | Owen says he can come in. |
| **The other Owen** | Bathroom mirror | Smiles a beat late; parts his hair the right way, which in a mirror is the wrong way. | Toothpaste on the glass. | Owen touches the glass, and the glass touches back. |

The **real deputy**: wet hat, radio crackling, badge reads right, doesn't ask to be invited; he just says "Sheriff's department, I'm coming in." The **real family**: Mom says "bug", Dad's watch on the left, Biscuit's red collar, and they're inside the iron.

## 8. Puzzles — CANON list, SUGGESTION mechanics

Each is one screen, a minute or less, and gives back a piece of story.

1. **The phone cord** (kitchen). The coiled cord is cut. Four little wires on each side (red, green, yellow, black), the far side jumbled. Click a wire, then its match, to twist them together. Then dial 9-1-1 on the keypad. The dispatcher answers; someone breathes on the upstairs extension. Sends a real deputy.
2. **The torn photo** (living room). Six pieces of the family photo from the mantel. Click two pieces to swap them until the picture is whole: the family on the porch, Biscuit in front in his **red** collar, Dad's watch on his left wrist.
3. **The basement key** (foyer). Carry the stepstool down from the bathroom, stand on it, and feel along the top of the door frame. Dad said where it was in the prologue.
4. **The attic** (hall). The hatch needs the hook pole from the parents' closet. You only get the pole by hiding in the closet from the fake Mom.

## 9. Open questions for Adam

2. Is it OK that dying resets the night (a loop, like Emit Time), or should each death be its own end card with a restart?

4. The family and pet names (Owen, Laura, Mike, Biscuit, Pepper, Mrs. Albright) are placeholders. Keep or change?
5. Should the real deputy ever be able to come down to the basement with you, for a sixth "everyone" ending?
