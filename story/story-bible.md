# Emit Time — Story Bible

**Status:** Draft v4. Built from Adam's story outline, updated with his decisions on resets, actions, difficulty, the scientist's identity, the setting, and Emit's motive and endings (13 Oct 2026).
**How to read this:** Sections marked **CANON** are Adam's story, kept as written. Anything marked **SUGGESTION** is a proposed addition or default and can be changed or dropped. Open questions for Adam are collected at the end.

## 1. Premise — CANON

You are the security guard working the front desk at a research facility. A scientist inside has just cracked time travel. From that moment on, people from other times start arriving at your desk, trying to get to the lab. Your job is to tell the legitimate visitors from the time travelers, without letting the travelers know you're onto them. Every mistake resets the day to the first flash, and you live it again.

## 1a. Setting and tone — CANON

- **Where:** a research lab called **Science Corp**.
- **When:** an ambiguous **1990s** era. This is the game's "present day."
- **Scene:** a security guard at a front desk, **at night**.
- **Style:** soft **film noir**.

### Setting details — SUGGESTION

- **Look:** a dim lobby lit by one desk lamp, rain on the glass doors, venetian-blind shadows, and the green glow of a CRT security monitor. The flashes outside light up the whole lobby for a moment, then it's dark again.
- **Desk props that double as tools:** a beige corded phone, a clipboard visitor list, a paper sign-in sheet, a Rolodex of staff extensions, and a wall calendar. The calendar gives the player a reference for the in-world date when checking paperwork.
- **Voice:** the guard narrates in short noir-style lines (*"Third flash tonight. The rain didn't care."*). Keep it soft: atmosphere and dry wit, not a parody.
- **Keep the 90s vague:** don't pin it to a year. Stick to tech everyone agrees is 90s (pagers, CRTs, VHS, early cell phones, fax machines, dial-up) so tells stay clear without fussing over exact dates.
- **Why visitors come at night:** late meetings, a deadline crunch, a delivery for the night shift, a worried family member. Noir makes the odd hours feel natural, and it means anyone who shows up at all is a little suspicious.

**Genre / influences:** click-based story RPG in the vein of *Papers, Please*, *Probably Stolen*, and *No, I'm Not a Human*: document-and-dialogue screening, mounting pressure, and a reveal that recasts everything you did.

## 2. Opening sequence — CANON

1. **The first flash.** There is a flash outside the building.
   - **Opening narration (CANON, Adam):** the guard narrates a stormy night that feels special. The lightning makes the hair on the back of his neck stand up. Then comes the big flash, and the guard makes his first wrong inference: that it's lightning. Later the player learns it was "the event," Emit arriving.
   - Draft lines are in [visitor-tells.md](visitor-tells.md#the-opening-narration--decided-direction-adam-draft-lines). On replays the narration is skippable, and the static on his neck becomes a cue that he's been here before.
2. **The scientist.** A disheveled scientist rushes in and tells you they've just made a massive breakthrough that will change the world: they've cracked the mysteries of time travel. He rushes back to the lab.
3. **The second flash.** Soon after, another flash. A clearly futuristic person comes to the desk and demands directions to the science lab, saying everyone there must be terminated.
4. **The choice.**
   - **Let him through:** the game loops back to the first flash.
   - **Shoot him:** the scientist comes back out.
5. **The briefing.** The scientist explains that now time travel exists, other travelers must be coming to stop it so they can control the future. He has real visitors coming (government agencies, other researchers, company officials) who must be let through, but you must screen carefully for future intruders. They will most likely try to infiltrate, so you shouldn't give away that you're aware of them.
6. **The cleanup.** He tells you to clean up the mess, says he has to continue his work, and rushes back to the lab. The screening game begins.

> **SUGGESTION:** The futuristic assassin works as the tutorial. It's the only visitor with no ambiguity at all, which teaches the player that the desk has a weapon, that choices reset the loop, and that the scientist is the one giving orders. Every later visitor should be harder than this one.

## 3. The loop / reset mechanic — CANON

- A wrong call (letting an intruder through, or shooting a legitimate visitor) resets you to the first flash.
- You re-live the loop from the start. **It's a hard reset: no checkpoints.**
- Any dialogue you've already heard in an earlier loop gets an **"I've heard this before — Skip"** button, so replays move quickly without giving up the hard reset.
- **The player WILL fail.** Living through the time loop is part of the game, not a punishment for bad play.

### What carries across loops — SUGGESTION (default)

| Carries over | Resets |
|---|---|
| The player's own memory of visitors, answers and tells | The guard's in-world state (the day, the body, the desk) |
| A **Guard's Notebook** that auto-logs what you learned about each visitor you've met (name, claims, tells you spotted) | Visitor order *within* known slots stays the same, so knowledge is useful |
| New dialogue options unlocked by past-loop knowledge (e.g. *"Your mother called ahead. What's her name again?"* after you learned it in a previous loop) | Any trust you earned with a visitor |
| A small **loop counter** visible only to the player (e.g. a tally scratched on the desk) | |

Rationale: the loop should feel like *Outer Wilds* / *Twelve Minutes*: dying isn't failure, it's research. Knowledge is the only progression, which also sets up the twist (the player eventually knows more than the scientist expects).

### Pacing and difficulty — CANON

- The first few visitors are easier. Difficulty ramps up over time.

### Pacing details — SUGGESTION

- **The skip button covers whole visitors too:** once a visitor's entire conversation has been heard, the player can skip straight to the decision. Decisions themselves are never skipped or made automatically, so every loop is still a real replay.
- **Some lines are skippable but changed:** a few visitors say something slightly different on later loops (a new detail, a reaction to the guard acting oddly). Those lines show no skip button, which tells the player to pay attention.
- **Designed failure points:** a few visitors around the middle and late game are built to fool first-time players (for example a perfect-looking android, or a legit visitor with a strong red herring). The answer only becomes clear with knowledge from an earlier loop.
- **Rough difficulty curve:** visitors 1–6 have obvious tells. Visitors 7–15 mix in red herrings and the first returning guests. Visitors 16–25 have subtle tells that need cross-checking. The final few lean on loop knowledge and twist clues.

## 4. Visitor screening — CANON

- Over the course of the game, many people come to the desk.
- You ask questions through **dialogue trees** and look at **context clues** to judge who is legitimate.
- Legitimate examples: pizza delivery guy, government official, a family member.
- Intruder examples: a person with a modern-style phone (a smartphone), a person in 1920s-style clothing.
- Intruders arrive from **critical times in history** (past and future), not only the future.

### Actions — CANON

- **Turn away**
- **Shoot**
- **Shutdown device:** the scientist gives the player a device for when androids or similar show up. It shuts them down.

### Returning guests — CANON

- Turned-away guests can come back later.
  - **Legitimate guests** return with urgent demands.
  - **Intruders** return in new disguises.

### Screening toolkit — SUGGESTION

Each visitor gives the player several things to cross-check, Papers, Please style:

- **Appearance:** clothing, accessories, hairstyle, items carried.
- **Paperwork:** ID badge, appointment letter, delivery slip. The scientist's **visitor list** on the desk is the reference document.
- **Dialogue:** a question tree (purpose of visit, who they're seeing, small talk that probes for era knowledge: prices, slang, current events, technology).
- **Desk tools** that unlock over the game: phone to call the lab or a company switchboard, a sign-in sheet (handwriting, dates written wrong), maybe a metal detector.
- **Actions at the end:** *Let through* plus the three canon actions (*Turn away*, *Shoot*, *Shutdown device*).

### Action rules — SUGGESTION (default)

| Action | On a legit visitor | On a human intruder | On an android |
|---|---|---|---|
| Let through | Correct | Reset | Reset |
| Turn away | Safe for now; they return with urgent demands. Turning them away a second time resets the loop (the meeting they needed never happens) | Safe for now; they return in a new disguise that is harder to spot | They return in a new disguise |
| Shoot | Reset | Correct | Doesn't work, or only stuns; the android reacts and the loop resets |
| Shutdown device | Nothing happens, and the visitor now knows you're suspicious (later dialogue gets guarded) | Nothing happens, and they know you're onto them | Correct |

- **When the device arrives:** the scientist hands it over at the start of the second third of the game, just before the first android. On every later loop he gives it at the same point, so it's part of the loop's script.
- **Twist hook:** the device is the scientist's own future technology. Late in the game, an observant player could try it on him (see Section 5).
- **Turning away is the cautious play** but not free: every intruder who comes back is harder to read, and legit guests who come back are angry and rushed.

Design rule: **every intruder should have at least two tells**, one visual and one in dialogue, so it's always fair. **Some legit visitors should carry a red herring** (an eccentric old researcher with a pocket watch, a teen with a retro phone as a fashion choice) so the player can't just pattern-match on looks.

Tone of the tells: intruders are trying hard to blend in, so the best ones are near-misses (right outfit, wrong decade of slang; perfect ID, wrong date format; knows the scientist's name but not his nickname).

## 5. Endgame and twist — CANON

- After **20–30 individuals**, the scientist comes out and tells you they're nearly done and you have to hold off just a bit longer.
- It is later shown that **the scientist is the first time traveler**.
- **He is an escaped prisoner from the future.**
- **His real name is Emit.** The player can find it on a **wanted poster near the 4th act**.
- **Dr. Aldous Kane is real.** Kane is the **first person to invent time travel**, here at Science Corp. **Emit needs time travel to be invented, but he needs to be the one controlling it.**
- **Fake Kane has been intercepting visitors** who would become a future problem for him, and "dealing" with them. **The warden can mention this.**
- That's **his motivation for coming back to this moment again and again**, and it's **why the security guard is special**.
- He eventually comes to the desk and says **the only way to stop this is to destroy time travel**. He's being truthful but not fully transparent: what he means is that it's the only way to stop the future authorities from hunting him. (Since the latest change, this is his fallback, not what he really wants. See Section 5b.)

## 5b. Emit's endings — CANON

There are two endings where Emit wins:

1. **You help Emit stop time travel from being invented.** He's stuck in the 90s, but nobody hunts him.
2. **You help Emit kill the real Dr. Kane.** You end up guarding him as he takes over the world, and time.

These sit alongside the true ending (shooting Emit at the first flash).

### How the two endings could play out — SUGGESTION (default)

- **Stranded:** this is the existing "Do it" choice at the 6:04 AM pitch. The fourth floor burns and the flashes stop. Emit stays as "Kane", a man with nowhere left to go. Final image: him at the window at dawn, older every loop the player didn't take.
- **Emit's guard:** this unlocks once the player knows the real Kane is upstairs (from the sister's description or the KANE, A. folders). At the pitch the player can say *"There's a better way. Kane's upstairs."* Emit smiles, and you let him take the stairs. Epilogue: years later, the same desk, now in a marble lobby with Emit's face on the wall. You're still the guard. The visitors are now people coming to beg him for things.
- **The intercepted visitors:** the warden's line could be *"Some of the people on that list never made it home. He's been seeing to it, one night at a time."* In the game, a few legit visitors you let up could go missing on later loops, and the notebook would show it.- There are hints that he has been time traveling and doing horrible things (crime, changing history, setting himself up as ruler, etc.), using **this moment in time, and you, as a safe base**.
- **To win the game, you shoot him when the loop starts again.**

### How the twist could work — SUGGESTION (default)

- **The first flash is Emit arriving.** The breakthrough is real, but it's Kane's, not his. Emit's speech borrows Kane's triumph as cover for why flashes and strangers have started appearing.
- **Emit poses as Kane to the guard.** The night guard has never met the reclusive Kane face to face, so Emit can claim to be him. Legitimate visitors who know the real Kane (his sister, his colleague, his patent lawyer) are the natural source of contradictions.
- **What "nearly done" means:** Emit is in the lab taking control of Kane's work: copying the notes, locking Kane out, and burning anything with Kane's name on it. If that fails, "destroy time travel" is the fallback.
- **Why the guard is special:** the guard controls the only way in or out of the lab at the moment time travel is born. Whoever holds the desk decides who reaches Kane. **CANON:** only the guard remembers the earlier loops, because he was standing nearest the first flash. The static he feels in the opening narration is the in-world sign of this. Emit needs the guard on his side, and every reset gives him another try.
- **The true ending, reframed:** shooting Emit when the loop restarts saves Kane. Time travel survives, and the authorities can finally catch Emit. Optional epilogue: the real Kane walks out to the desk for the first time.
- **The future intruders are the authorities hunting him:** prison wardens, bounty hunters, and timeline police. The futuristic "terminator" in the tutorial is one of them, blunt but not wrong.
- **The past-era intruders are what's left of his crimes:** people from moments in history he changed or ruled, plus agents in hastily researched disguises who landed in the wrong decade. This explains why visitors from the 1920s or the Middle Ages would want to get to the lab.
- **Safe base:** this night at Science Corp is the one point in history the authorities can't skip past, because time travel doesn't fully exist yet. That makes it Emit's safe house. Each time you fail, he gets another clean night, which is why he never seems worried about the resets.
- **"Destroy time travel":** this is his pitch near the end, and the second-best result for him. Helping him leads to the "Stranded" ending (Section 5b). What he really wants is to own the invention.

Suggested reveal path, built from clues the player can gather across loops so the final loop feels earned rather than random:

1. **Seeded early:** the scientist is "disheveled" in a way that doesn't fit someone who just walked out of a lab (scorched coat, futuristic stitching, a watch that shows the wrong year). A sharp-eyed player can spot it on loop 1.
2. **Intruder whispers:** a few intruders, if questioned the right way or if you've caught them, drop lines like *"You don't even know who you work for"*, *"He wasn't here yesterday, was he?"*, or *"Prisoner 0-1. Escaped custody, year 2240."*
2a. **Hints of his crimes:** a past-era visitor carries a coin or portrait with the scientist's face as a king. The wanted poster for *Emit* is the payoff (near act 4, per canon). It could fall out of a future agent's coat or be pinned to a bounty hunter's clipboard. A history book a legit researcher is carrying has a chapter that changes between loops.
3. **Legit visitor contradictions:** a real colleague or family member doesn't recognise the scientist's description, or the visitor list was written in his handwriting with a date that hasn't happened yet.
4. **"Hold off a bit longer":** at the 20–30 mark, his speech reveals he knows things he couldn't (names of visitors he never met, events from previous loops). He is aware of the loop.
5. **The device clue:** using the shutdown device on the scientist does nothing (he's human), but it makes him flinch and he asks where you got it, even though he gave it to you. A player who tries this learns he remembers the earlier loops.
6. **The final loop:** armed with all of this, the player is offered (or simply has) the choice to shoot the scientist the moment he rushes in after the first flash. That's the win.

> **SUGGESTION:** The *Shoot* button is always available on the scientist from loop 1. Shooting him mid-night just resets the loop (he triggers the Tether as he falls; see Section 5a). Shooting him right after the first flash only triggers the true ending once the Notebook holds the key clues (the wanted poster, the wrong-year watch, and the warden's file). Before that, the guard hesitates and Emit hurries past. This keeps the twist hidden but the solution in plain sight.

## 5a. Epilogue: why the loop resets — CANON

- An epilogue explains **why letting intruders through resets time**: it's something **Emit set up to keep himself from being taken**.

### How the reset works — SUGGESTION (default)

- **The Tether:** Emit wears a stolen prison recall device disguised as a wristwatch. It's the watch showing the wrong year from twist clue 1, so the answer has been on screen since loop 1. The Tether is anchored to the moment he arrived, the first flash.
- **The trigger:** if a hunter from his own time gets within reach of him, the Tether snaps time back to its anchor. That covers wardens, bounty hunters, androids, and people from history he wronged, since they all carry future tech that tracks him. This is why every intruder you let through ends the night. They reach the lab, he triggers the Tether, and it's the first flash again.
- **The other resets:** shooting a legit visitor brings police, a lockdown and a crime scene to the desk, which ruins his safe base. The Tether also fires when the building is compromised. Turning a legit guest away twice means a meeting Kane needed never happens, which strands Emit's plan, so he resets to try again.
- **Why the guard remembers:** he stands nearest the first flash, the Tether's anchor point (canon). Every time it snaps back, it runs through him.
- **Why Emit never worries:** the guard is his gatekeeper and the Tether is his safety net. As far as he knows, every loop the guard fails just gives him another clean night.
- **Why the final shot works:** if Emit dies right at the anchor moment, the Tether has nowhere earlier to send him. The loop closes for good.

### The epilogue scene — SUGGESTION (default)

1. **Morning.** The storm has passed. Emit is on the lobby floor and the Tether on his wrist is sparking.
2. **The warden arrives,** the one from roster #30, who couldn't get through before. He takes the Tether and explains it in a few dry lines: *"Recall anchor. Prison issue, stolen. Every time one of us got near him, he pulled the whole night back like a tablecloth. You were standing on the tablecloth."*
3. **The tally.** The warden glances at the loop tally scratched into the desk and says the real number out loud. That's the count of the player's actual failures, so the game turns the player's resets into the story.
4. **Kane walks out.** The real Dr. Aldous Kane comes to the desk for the first time, alive, his work intact. He has no idea what the guard did for him.
5. **Last narration:** something like *"Lightning, I'd told myself. It was never lightning."* That pays off the guard's first wrong inference from the opening.

> **Note:** shooting Emit at any other point in the night doesn't stick. He triggers the Tether as he falls, and the player sees a flash they now recognise. The shot only ends the loop right after the first flash, when he's standing at the anchor point. This replaces my earlier "nobody believes you" idea (see the updated note in Section 5).

## 8. Glossary

- **First flash:** the loop start point. The guard first takes it for lightning, but it's "the event": Emit arriving from the future.
- **Emit:** the scientist's real name, an escaped prisoner from the future.
- **Dr. Aldous Kane:** the real first inventor of time travel, working in the lab at Science Corp.
- **Science Corp:** the 90s research lab where the game takes place.
- **Loop:** one run of the day from the first flash to a mistake or the endgame.
- **Tell:** a visual or dialogue clue that a visitor is from another time.
- **Red herring:** a legitimate visitor with an apparently suspicious detail.
- **Visitor list:** the scientist's list of expected legitimate visitors at the desk.
